# On the coupling between membrane bending and stretching in lipid vesicles

## Summary
Shows that heating causes membrane stretching which decreases bending rigidity via thickness coupling: $\kappa(A) = \kappa_0 h^\alpha$ with $\alpha = 2$-$3$. This means that as a membrane is heated and stretches, it also becomes softer — facilitating further deformation. For DOPC, $\kappa \approx 20$-$30\,k_BT$ with area expansion modulus $\lambda = 0.1$-$0.3$ J/m$^2$.

## Key Findings
- Bending rigidity decreases with membrane stretching: $\kappa \propto h^{2\text{-}3}$
- For GUVs: relative area expansion $\hat{A} = (A-A_0)/A_0 \approx 3\alpha\kappa/(2R_0^2 \lambda)$
- DOPC bilayer thickness: $h \approx 5$ nm
- Area expansion modulus: $\lambda = 0.1$-$0.3$ J/m$^2$
- Thermal stretching → softer membrane → easier deformation (positive feedback)

## Relevance to My Research

### Direct Applications
- **Positive feedback mechanism:** heating → stretching → softening → more deformation
- This could explain why deformations are much larger during heating onset than offset
- The asymmetric onset/offset dynamics I observe may result from this nonlinear softening
- Provides theoretical basis for why rapid heating produces stronger shape changes than gradual
