
## Summary: Käs & Sackmann 1991

The paper systematically catalogs temperature-driven shape transitions of DMPC and POPC GUVs in pure water (and some osmolyte conditions), working within the **bilayer coupling model** of Svetina & Zeks. The control parameter is the reduced area-to-volume ratio, tuned via the high thermal expansivity of the membrane in the $L_\alpha$ phase. Key content:

**Theory backbone.** The trajectory in the $(v, \Delta a)$ phase diagram upon heating follows:

$$\Delta a(v) = \Delta a(v_0) + b\left[\left(\frac{v_0}{v}\right)^{2/3} - 1\right]$$

with $b = A^{in}(T_0)/8\pi d(T_0)[A^{in}(T_0)/4\pi]^{1/2}$ and the asymmetry parameter $\gamma$ (difference in thermal expansivities of the two monolayers). The trajectory slope in the phase diagram is entirely controlled by $\gamma$ — positive $\gamma$ drives pear/outward budding; negative $\gamma$ drives stomatocyte/invagination; $\gamma \approx 0$ gives reentrant dumbbell transitions.

**Thermal expansivity numbers.** $\alpha_\text{DMPC} = 6\times10^{-3}$ K$^{-1}$, $\alpha_\text{POPC} = 3.5\times10^{-3}$ K$^{-1}$.

**Key experimental observations:**

1. All reversible shape changes (types 1–3) are consistent with bilayer coupling; pear and stomatocyte shapes are stable over wide temperature ranges — contradicting the spontaneous curvature model.
2. Type 4 (blebbing/tether formation from near-spherical vesicles with only ~6.7% excess area increase): the membrane becomes "edgy," flickering ceases, then tethers/blebs erupt. This cannot be explained by either model — attributed to pre-existing hidden folds and tethers adhered to the shell.
3. **Irreversibility and volume loss during cooling**: explicitly measured (Figs. 5b, 9b). They observe 8–20% volume reduction attributed to transient pore formation.
4. **Sucrose note (p. 831):** "in sucrose solutions even at smaller concentrations one occasionally observes the formation of very thin threads extending into the vesicle interior. In this case the vesicle exhibited a spherical non-flickering shape."
5. Pretreatment (whether the vesicle was held under lateral tension before heating) critically determines which transition type occurs — attributing $\gamma$ selection to stress-accelerated flip-flop.

## Connections to Your Work

This paper is strikingly relevant in several ways that go beyond being a general reference — it gives mechanistic language for phenomena you're already observing:

**1. Your "type 4" analog.** The transition where flickering suddenly stops and morphological instabilities appear is essentially what you're seeing at the onset of your heating events. The slope transition from $\approx -3$ (baseline) to $\approx -0.11$ (Post-heat 1) — meaning the spectrum flattens to noise — corresponds physically to the membrane being driven into the near-spherical high-tension regime where thermal fluctuations are quenched. The "edgy, non-flickering" description in their paper is your Post-heat 1 regime in spectral language.

**2. Volume loss mechanism.** Their measurement of 8–20% volume reduction during cooling via transient pore formation is the microscopic process behind the osmotic contribution to your partial post-heat tension recovery. The key phrase: "local pore formation took place during the cooling... most likely at temperatures where the number of small vesicles is reduced." In your system, each heating cycle drives the vesicle transiently toward a limiting shape (budding/invagination threshold), and partial volume loss at that point creates a new baseline reduced volume $v_0$ for the next cycle — driving progressive tension accumulation.

**3. The sucrose observation is critical.** They report that even small sucrose concentrations produce internal thread formation and spherical non-flickering shapes, which is exactly the morphological state of your gold-substrate GUVs during heating. At 200 mM sucrose you are well above whatever threshold they observed — so the osmolarity mismatch kinetics (your $\tau_\text{perm}$ argument) are compounded by this sucrose-specific behavior.

**4. The $\alpha_A$ value.** Their $\alpha_\text{POPC} = 3.5\times10^{-3}$ K$^{-1}$ is close to your assumed $\alpha_A \approx 3\times10^{-3}$ K$^{-1}$ for DOPC, which is reassuring. DOPC and POPC are both diunsaturated/monounsaturated $L_\alpha$-phase lipids at room temperature with similar chain packing, so the agreement is expected.

**5. The $\gamma$-pretreatment coupling** directly maps onto your cycle-to-cycle irreversibility. Their argument is that lateral stress accelerates flip-flop and establishes an asymmetric $\Delta A$ that persists. In your system, each optothermal pulse subjects the membrane to rapid, high thermal stress — almost certainly pushing $|\gamma|$ away from zero, which shifts the phase trajectory toward invagination-type dynamics. The "history dependence" they describe phenomenologically is your inter-cycle tension buildup expressed in bending-spectroscopy language.
