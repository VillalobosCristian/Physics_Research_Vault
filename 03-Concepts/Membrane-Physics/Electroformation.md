# Electroformation

**Electroformation** is the most common method for preparing [[Giant Unilamellar Vesicles (GUVs)]].

## Principle

1. Deposit lipid film on conductive surface (ITO glass)
2. Hydrate with aqueous solution
3. Apply AC electric field
4. Lipid bilayers swell and detach as GUVs

## Protocol Overview

### Lipid Deposition
- Dissolve lipids in chloroform (~1 mg/mL)
- Spread on ITO-coated glass slides
- Dry under vacuum (>2 hours)

### Electroswelling
- Assemble chamber with spacer (~1 mm)
- Fill with hydration solution (sucrose ~200 mM)
- Apply AC field: ~1-3 V, 10 Hz
- Duration: 1-3 hours at room temperature (or above Tm)

### Harvesting
- Gently pipette GUVs from chamber
- Transfer to observation solution (glucose, osmotically matched)

## Key Parameters

| Parameter | Typical Value | Effect |
|-----------|---------------|--------|
| Voltage | 1-3 V | Higher → larger GUVs |
| Frequency | 10 Hz | Lower → gentler swelling |
| Temperature | > Tm | Required for fluid phase |
| Ionic strength | < 10 mM | High salt inhibits formation |

## Advantages

- Reproducible GUV production
- Good size distribution (10-100 μm)
- Works for many lipid compositions

## Limitations

- Requires low ionic strength
- ITO surfaces needed
- Some lipids form poorly

## Alternatives

- **Gentle hydration** — No electric field, slower
- **Inverted emulsion** — For encapsulation
- **Microfluidic** — Precise size control
- **Gel-assisted** — PVA or agarose substrates
