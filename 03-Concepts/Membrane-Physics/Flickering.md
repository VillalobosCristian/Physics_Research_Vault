# Flickering

**Flickering** refers to the visible, thermally-driven shape fluctuations of [[Giant Unilamellar Vesicles (GUVs)]] and other soft membranes.

## Origin

Flickering arises from [[thermal fluctuations]] of the membrane:
- Thermal energy $k_BT$ excites bending modes
- Low [[bending rigidity]] (~10-20 $k_BT$) allows visible undulations
- Amplitude depends on $\kappa$ and [[surface tension]]

## Observation

### What You See
- Membrane contour "wobbles" continuously
- Undulations on μm length scale
- Timescale: milliseconds to seconds

### Microscopy
- Phase contrast: High contrast at membrane edge
- Fluorescence: Direct membrane visualization
- DIC: Good for thick samples

## Quantification

Flickering is analyzed via [[fluctuation spectroscopy]]:

1. Track membrane contour over time
2. Decompose into Fourier modes
3. Calculate [[membrane fluctuation spectrum]]
4. Extract mechanical properties

## Historical Note

First observed in red blood cells ("RBC flickering") in the 1890s. Later understood as thermal fluctuations by Brochard & Lennon (1975).

## Amplitude Estimate

For a membrane patch of size $L$:

$$\langle u^2 \rangle \sim \frac{k_BT L^2}{\kappa}$$

For $\kappa = 20 k_BT$ and $L = 10$ μm: $\langle u^2 \rangle^{1/2} \sim 1$ μm
