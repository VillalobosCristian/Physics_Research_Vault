# A hydro-thermophoretic trap for microparticles near a gold-coated substrate

## Summary
Demonstrates quasi-3D trapping of microparticles using two IR laser hotspots on a 30 $\mu$m gold-coated glass substrate. Uses upconversion nanoparticles as nanothermometers to directly measure local temperature. Reports modest temperature rises ($\Delta T \approx 4.6$-$5.6$ K) and trap stiffness $\sim 40$ pN/$\mu$m, with successful trapping of Dictyostelium cells without damage.

## Key Findings
- Temperature at hotspots: $302.6$ K ($\Delta T = 4.6$ K) at optimized laser powers
- Trap stiffness: $\sim 40$ pN/$\mu$m (harmonic behavior)
- Can trap biological cells ($\sim 10\,\mu$m) without damage at $\Delta T \sim 1°$C
- Trap center tunable by adjusting laser power ratio
- Particles exhibit out-of-plane rotation indicating fluid flow contribution
