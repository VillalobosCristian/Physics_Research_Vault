## New Ideas — directions suggested by the curated set

1. **Map the non-equilibrium (v(t), Δa(t)) trajectory onto the Seifert–Lipowsky diagram.** Static phase diagrams (seifert-lipowsky 1995; miao-seifert 1994) are well characterized, but *dynamic* trajectories crossing them are not. Your real-time data could be the first experimental non-equilibrium path — a clean, citable novelty (already flagged "desirable" in your gap analysis).

2. **Frame the transient permeability spike as a fluid-phase analog of Leirer's phase-transition expulsion.** leirer 2009 gives τ ≈ 4–5 s for gel↔fluid expulsion; if your fluid-phase DOPC shows comparable timescales at modest ΔT, that contrast is the paper's strongest mechanistic hook. Pair with the calcein assay (Deep Dive note) and wennerström 2022 osmotic-stability theory.

3. **Cholesterol / POPC as a mechanism discriminator.** méléard-gerbeaud 1997 and gracià 2010 quantify how sterols stiffen membranes and cut permeability. If deformation is abolished by 30% cholesterol, that cleanly separates a permeability mechanism from a pure thermal-softening one — a decisive control figure.

4. **Active matter inside the optothermal trap (next project).** Your droplet active-bath work (villalobos-concha liu 2025) plus willems 2025 (run-and-tumble active GUVs), park 2022, and le nagard 2022 suggest a natural follow-up: encapsulate swimmers/active colloids in DOPC GUVs and use the gold-substrate heating to switch activity or membrane tension on demand — optothermal control of an active vesicle.

5. **Couple optothermal heating to particle wrapping/uptake.** ayala 2023 shows membrane *fluctuations* gate particle uptake, and sirch 2024 shows uptake is *phase-state dependent*. Since your heating modulates both κ and (hypothesized) permeability, you could drive wrapping/uptake optothermally — connecting the wrapping literature (deserno; spanke 2020; fessler) to your platform.

6. **Time-resolved κ(t), σ(t) via sliding-window flicker analysis.** Combine pécréaux 2004 + rautu 2017 corrections with faizi-granek 2024 dynamics to track the tension–bending crossover mode n* = √σ̄ through the heating cycle — turning a static κ measurement into a dynamic readout of the membrane's thermal response.

7. **Thermo-osmotic flow as an active stress on the membrane.** fränzl 2022 and the slip-theory papers (tsuji 2024/2025; ganti 2017) quantify gold-substrate thermo-osmosis. Estimating the resulting flow-induced stress on a nearby vesicle (seifert 1999; lebedev 2008 membrane-in-flow theory) could provide an alternative/complementary driving mechanism to test against the osmotic one.
