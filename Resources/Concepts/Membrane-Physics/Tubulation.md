# Tubulation

**Tubulation** is the formation of cylindrical membrane tubes (tethers) from vesicles or flat membranes.

## Tube Geometry

A membrane tube of radius $r$ has:
- Mean curvature: $H = 1/(2r)$
- Gaussian curvature: $K = 0$ (cylindrical)

## Equilibrium Tube Radius

From [[Helfrich free energy]] minimization:

$$r_0 = \sqrt{\frac{\kappa}{2\sigma}}$$

Where:
- $\kappa$ = [[bending rigidity]]
- $\sigma$ = [[surface tension]]

## Force-Extension

The force to pull a tube from a GUV:

$$f = 2\pi\sqrt{2\kappa\sigma}$$

This is independent of tube length (for long tubes).

## Tube Formation Methods

1. **Optical tweezers** — Pull bead attached to membrane
2. **Micropipette** — Aspirate and extend
3. **Molecular motors** — Kinesin walking on microtubules
4. **Flow** — Hydrodynamic shear
5. **Thermal gradients** — Spontaneous tubulation

## Applications

- **Measuring $\kappa$ and $\sigma$** — From force measurements
- **Intracellular transport** — Tube-based trafficking
- **Membrane area regulation** — Tube as area reservoir

## Dynamics

Tube pulling dynamics depend on:
- Membrane viscosity
- Intermonolayer friction
- Lipid diffusion (for area supply)
